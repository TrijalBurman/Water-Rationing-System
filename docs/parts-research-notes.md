## Consequences for the build

- **The two 9 V batteries do not produce 12 V directly.** Series produces nominal 18 V; parallel remains nominal 9 V. Do not connect either arrangement directly to this 12 V valve. The supplied photograph identifies small rectangular PP3/6F22 batteries (NIPPO heavy-duty and Hi-Watt general-purpose); their sustained current capability does not establish suitability for the listed 1 A valve load. A regulated 12 V supply with adequate continuous current, an appropriate fuse, and valve-coil suppression is needed. Code cannot compensate for the missing supply.
- **The valve is a hydraulic limitation too.** The seller explicitly excludes gravity feed. Its stated 0.02 MPa lower operating pressure corresponds mathematically to about 2.04 m of static water head, before pipe/flow losses; that conversion does not certify a gravity installation. A small tabletop two-tank setup needs a suitable zero-pressure valve or an appropriately engineered pressure source. No pump is in the user's inventory.
- **Do not build a 5 V-to-ESP32 divider from one 1 kΩ and one 150 Ω resistor.** With 150 Ω above and 1 kΩ below, a 5 V signal becomes about 4.35 V; reversed, about 0.65 V. Neither is suitable. Three 1 kΩ resistors can form a 1 kΩ upper / 2 kΩ lower divider if enough were received, but the divider must be designed for actual maximum signal voltage and tolerances. The two flow outputs and ultrasonic echo need individually verified 3.3 V interfaces.
- The YF-S201 listing describes a square-wave output but does not establish whether the delivered sensor has a pull-up to 5 V, is open collector without an internal pull-up, or is actively driven. Do not infer that yellow is safe to connect directly to ESP32.
- The YF-S201 listing also gives approximately 2.25 mL per pulse, slightly inconsistent with its frequency formula. Use 450 pulses/L as a starting value, then calibrate the two sensors independently with measured water volume. Values below the 1 L/min stated flow range are not guaranteed by this listing.
- Only one valve is present. The second relay channel must remain unused/off; a two-channel relay does not create a second controlled water path.
- A normally closed float can be installed so normal level closes the contact and full level opens it. A pull-up input then reports a high state on full level or a disconnected cable. Confirm this with a continuity test; “NC” alone does not establish the installed water-level orientation. It cannot detect a shorted cable or a stuck float.

## Relay trigger information and remaining unknowns

The exact relay page links a [seller trigger-voltage table](https://robu-prod-media.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/product-descriptions/2021/11/1121234-1.jpg). Visually read values for its 5 V version:

| Selection | Relay on | Relay off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low-level trigger | 0–2 V | 3–5 V |
| High-level trigger | 3.3–5 V | 0–2.5 V |

This is a seller chart, not a verified input schematic. It does not specify input current, pull-up paths, disconnected-input state, or ESP32 power-off backfeed. Nominal 3.3 V sits at the chart's minimum high-trigger threshold, so it is not evidence of adequate noise margin. Select the correct trigger mode and use a verified interface whose GPIO side stays within ESP32 ratings. Confirm actual startup and shutdown behavior with the valve disconnected before enabling water control.

The unknowns requiring physical verification are: exact ESP32 board/USB-power arrangement, battery load capability, number of resistors received, relay board markings/jumper position and input circuitry, AJ-SR04M firmware/mode setting, measured tank geometry and ultrasonic headroom (reported capacities 2.5/1.5 L, source depth approximately 20 cm), float orientation, and valve-coil resistance/current and pressure suitability. No software change can validate those physical properties.
